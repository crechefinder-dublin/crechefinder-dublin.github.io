What to Pack for Creche: A Practical Checklist for Dublin Parents

A practical checklist of what to pack for creche, from nappies and spare clothes to rain gear, sun protection and comfort items, plus tips on labelling and creche policies.

Getting your child's bag ready for creche can feel like packing for a small expedition, especially in the early weeks. Every creche has its own list and policies, so always start with what your provider asks for. That said, most lists share a lot in common.

This checklist covers the items Dublin parents most often need, with a few tips to save time and avoid lost property. Use it alongside your creche's own guidance and adjust it for your child's age.

The checklist

The everyday bag

  • Spare clothes, ideally two full changes
  • Spare socks and underwear for children who are toilet training
  • A labelled water bottle or beaker
  • Any comfort item, such as a soft toy or blanket

For babies and toddlers

  • Nappies and wipes, unless the creche provides them
  • Nappy cream, if your child uses one
  • Bottles, formula or expressed milk as agreed with staff
  • A sleeping bag or blanket for naps if requested

Weather gear

  • Waterproof jacket and trousers or an all-in-one suit
  • Wellies for outdoor play
  • A warm hat and gloves in winter
  • A sun hat and sun cream in summer, if the creche allows it

Paperwork

  • Signed medication forms where needed
  • Details of any allergies or dietary needs
  • Up-to-date emergency contact information
  • Your list of authorised collectors
01

Label absolutely everything

Creches care for many children with similar clothes, bottles and coats. Labels make it far more likely that everything comes home. Iron-on or stick-on name labels and a permanent marker are well worth the effort. Label shoes, hats and gloves too, as these are the items most often mislaid.

02

Dress for play, not for photos

Creche is busy, creative and often messy. Choose comfortable, washable clothes that your child can move freely in, and avoid anything precious. Clothes that are easy to pull up and down help children who are learning to use the toilet, and shoes with velcro rather than laces encourage independence.

03

Dublin weather means outdoor gear

Most creches value outdoor time and will take children outside in all but the worst weather. Good waterproofs and wellies mean your child can enjoy the garden even on a damp day. Many creches like families to leave a set of wet-weather gear on site so it is always available.

  • Ask whether you can leave wellies and rain gear at creche.
  • Check the gear still fits each season; children grow quickly.
  • Pack layers rather than one heavy coat.
04

Food and allergies

Many full-day creches provide meals and snacks, while others ask parents to send food. Follow your creche's food policy carefully, particularly around nuts and other allergens, as rules are there to protect children with allergies. Tell staff in writing about any allergies or intolerances and update them if anything changes.

05

Medication and health

If your child needs medication at creche, you will usually be asked to complete a consent form and to supply the medicine in its original packaging with the pharmacy label. Ask about the creche's illness policy too, so you know when your child should stay at home.

06

Keep a spare-kit routine

Each evening, check the bag for wet or dirty clothes and restock. A quick routine, such as refilling spares while dinner cooks, saves a stressful morning scramble. Some parents keep a ready-packed bag of spare clothes by the door, so restocking is a two-minute job.

07

What to leave at home

Some things are best kept out of the creche bag. Valuable toys can be lost or cause upsets when other children want to play with them, and small items can be a choking risk for babies in the room. Jewellery, sweets and anything with small detachable parts are usually discouraged. If in doubt, ask staff before sending something new in with your child.

!

Tip: Ask for the creche's own list

Before you buy anything, ask your creche for its packing list and policies. Some provide nappies, wipes, bedding or sun cream, and some do not allow certain items, so checking first saves money and hassle.

A well-packed bag means one less thing to think about. Before long, packing for creche becomes second nature for the whole family.
